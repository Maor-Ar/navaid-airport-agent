# Anchorage (ANC) qualitative notes

T-100 is **segment** traffic, not true origin-destination. Cargo is out of
passenger long-haul unless the analyst asks for cargo.

title: ANC long-haul mix is cargo-heavy; measure passenger segments separately
---

Ted Stevens Anchorage International (ANC) is a technical-stop and **cargo**
hub on transpacific routes. A naive long-haul share on mixed T-100 will look
huge because freighters and passenger widebodies both fly **long-haul**
distances. For the assignment question (percentage of long-haul flights out
of Anchorage) the default is passenger **segments**, Eurocontrol **>4000 km**,
with **cargo out unless asked**.

Also report **international share** and optional percent of segments **over
6 hours**. Pin the geography: ANC→JFK is about 5420 km / 3370 mi, which is
long-haul on the kilometer rule. ANC→SEA is a shortish domestic segment;
an ANC–SEA–JFK passenger is two T-100 segments, not one true O&D.

Do not quote a cargo-inclusive mix as if it were the passenger long-haul
answer. If cargo is requested, label it cargo. Long haul, long-haul, and
greater than 4000 km are the same retrieval idea.

title: ANC cargo versus passenger — do not blend the shares
---

Anchorage **cargo** operations (freighter through-load, fuel/tech stops)
are a different business from **passenger** terminal expansion. High cargo
long-haul activity does not by itself make a passenger concourse profitable.
Keep cargo vs passenger split in any ANC brief: seats and passengers for
the passenger long-haul share; freight tons only when asked.

Terminal investment thesis at ANC is not "it has a lot of long-haul metal
on the ramp." Ask whether passenger demand is landside-constrained, whether
the mix is transferring cargo, and whether T-100 segments overstate true
passenger O&D.
