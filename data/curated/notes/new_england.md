# New England qualitative notes

New England = CT, ME, MA, NH, RI, VT. PWM is Portland **Maine**, not PDX
(Portland, Oregon). Peer-relative: biggest is not automatically the best
terminal-expansion candidate.

title: New England — BOS is scale; regionals can be landside-bound
---

In New England the scale airport is Boston Logan (**BOS**). Bradley (**BDL**),
T.F. Green (**PVD**), Portland Maine (**PWM**), and Manchester-Boston (**MHT**)
are the landside-constrained regionals that can be stronger **terminal
expansion** candidates than Logan even though they are smaller.

Logan already has hub scale. Its bottlenecks are often airfield, airspace,
and a tight seaport site — more concourse does not mint slots. The regionals
more often bind on **landside**: gates, holdrooms, security, bag claim, curb.
That is the renovation thesis in the brief (unlock flight and passenger
capacity inside the building).

Peer-relative scoring inside {BOS, BDL, PVD, PWM, MHT, BTV, BGR, ORH} is
required. Do not answer "which New England airports are strong candidates"
with "BOS because it is biggest." Burlington (BTV), Bangor (BGR), and
Worcester (ORH) belong in the set; they are smaller but still New England.

title: BOS Logan is the New England scale airport
---

Boston Logan (BOS) is the New England **scale** hub: by far the most
enplanements, the most long-haul and international mix, and the most
absolute delay. Scale is not the same as a landside terminal-expansion
opportunity. Logan's site, runways, and airspace mean many constraints are
**airside**. Use BOS as the peer-set ceiling, not as the default "invest
here" answer.

title: BDL Bradley — landside regional versus BOS scale
---

Bradley International (BDL, Hartford / Springfield) is a New England
**landside** regional. Catchment is Connecticut and western Massachusetts,
with leakage to BOS on connecting and high-frequency markets. If gates,
holdrooms, or curb bind before the airfield does, terminal capex can raise
throughput. Contrast with Logan scale: BDL can out-compete BOS as an
expansion candidate precisely because the bottleneck is the building.

title: PVD T.F. Green — landside regional, leakage toward BOS
---

Rhode Island T.F. Green (PVD, Providence) is a **landside**-leaning
regional in the Boston leakage field. Many metro passengers already choose
BOS for network depth. PVD expansion is about serving the local catchment
in the building (gates, security, curb), not matching Logan's airside
scale. Keep PVD in the New England peer set with BDL, PWM, and MHT.

title: PWM Portland Maine — landside regional, not PDX

Portland International Jetport (**PWM**, Portland **Maine**) is a small
New England regional. It is not PDX (Portland, Oregon). PWM is typically
**landside**-constrained relative to its airfield: terminal, gates, and
curb matter more than a second runway. Include PWM when the user says
"add PWM" to a New England set. Do not drop it because BOS is larger.

Manchester-Boston Regional (MHT) sits in Logan's leakage shadow: New
Hampshire and northern Massachusetts passengers use MHT when fare, parking,
or access beat BOS. That is a **landside** regional story (gates, curb,
holdrooms) versus BOS scale. Keep MHT with BDL, PVD, and PWM as the
New England regionals that can win a terminal-expansion screen.
